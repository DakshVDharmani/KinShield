01 · THE BASICS4 MIN

Could that video be fake?

A deepfake is media made or changed with AI to imitate a person or event. The word is often used loosely; the important question is what the media claims and where it came from.

Made

A model generates a new image, voice, video, or text from learned patterns. It does not retrieve a hidden recording of the event.

Changed

A real recording may be edited, face-swapped, dubbed, or lip-synced. A fabricated screenshot can also pair real words with a false context.

Misleadingly shared

Authentic footage can be cropped, old, or captioned to imply something that did not happen. A real clip can still tell a false story.

Common misconception: "A strange blink or hand proves a fake."

There is no universal visual tell. Compression, lighting, motion blur, and editing can create odd artifacts in authentic media too. A polished fake may show none. Treat visual clues as a reason to check context, never as proof.

02 · HOW PRESSURE WORKS4 MIN

How could a child be targeted?

AI can make impersonation and fabrication easier. Many underlying harms—bullying, grooming, scams, coercion, and privacy violations—also happen without AI.

01

Borrowed identity

A copied voice or familiar profile asks for money, a login code, secrecy, or an urgent favour. Urgency is a cue to verify separately.

02

Fabricated "proof"

An edited image, fake screenshot, or misleading clip is circulated to embarrass or isolate someone. A screenshot can be fabricated; it does not establish a conversation happened.

03

Threats and sexual exploitation

Someone may claim to have an intimate image, demand money or more images, or threaten to share manipulated material. The child is not at fault, whether the material is real, edited, or invented.

04

False relationships and misinformation

Fake profiles, synthetic identities, deceptive influencer endorsements, fabricated news, and companion bots can blur who is speaking and what is reliable.

What not to assume

A child going quiet, changing apps, or becoming upset is not proof of exploitation. Ask gently and privately. Focus on what they experienced and whether they feel safe.

03 · PAUSE AND VERIFY3 MIN

What deserves a second look?

No single sign proves a message is fake. Look for a mismatch between the request, the sender, and what you can confirm independently.

Suspicious request

Unexpected urgency, secrecy, money, passwords, or a verification code.

Unfamiliar route

A "known" person asks from a new account, number, or channel.

Missing context

No original source, date, location, or independent confirmation.

Pressure to spread

"Share now," "don't tell anyone," or threats if a child asks for help.

PAUSE→VERIFY→SUPPORT→REPORT

Call a trusted person using a number already saved. Find the original publisher. Do not forward unverified content. For abusive material involving a child, preserve non-content details such as usernames, URLs, timestamps, and report IDs when safe and lawful; do not download or redistribute the image.

04 · THE HUMAN RESPONSE4 MIN

What if my child tells me?

A child may worry about friendships, reputation, privacy, or being blamed. The first response can make it easier—or harder—to ask for help again.

01

Listen without blame. "I'm glad you told me. This isn't your fault."

02

Check immediate safety. Ask if anyone is in immediate danger or demanding something now.

03

Keep the circle small. Avoid interrogating, publicly confronting, or threatening to take away every device as an immediate punishment.

04

Get support together. Agree on a trusted adult, school safeguarding contact, platform report, or local authority as appropriate.

PRACTISE A FIRST SENTENCE

05 · THE PRACTICAL PLAYBOOK5 MIN

What do I do right now?

Choose the closest situation. This guide is general information; local services and reporting routes vary. If someone faces immediate physical danger, contact local emergency services now.

FIRST RESPONSE

A suspicious voice or message

  1. Do not pay, share a code, or click a link in the message.
  2. Contact the person through a number or channel you already trust.
  3. If a child is involved, tell them you will check together and they are not in trouble.

If money was sent, contact your bank promptly and use your country's official cybercrime reporting route.

If sexual exploitation may be involved

Do not ask your child to resend or show intimate imagery. Do not download, screenshot, copy, or forward it. If safe and lawful, keep non-content details: account names, profile links, URLs, timestamps, and platform report numbers. 07 · EVERYDAY AI LITERACY4 MIN

How do we talk about AI?

These are suggested teaching bands, not fixed stages. Start with the child's questions and build autonomy as they grow.

AGES 5–8

Notice and ask

"Pictures can be made up, like stories. Let's ask where this came from." Practise checking with a trusted adult before acting on surprising messages.

AGES 9–12

Check and share carefully

Compare a claim with a second trusted source. Talk about consent before posting someone's face or voice, and name two adults they can tell.

AGES 13–17

Identity, consent, and independence

Discuss privacy settings, reputational pressure, impersonation, intimate image threats, and how to seek help while preserving the teen's voice in the plan.
